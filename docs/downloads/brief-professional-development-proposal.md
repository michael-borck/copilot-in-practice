# Sample brief: professional development proposal

> Sanitised sample for the Copilot session. Client identity, staff names, rates and totals have been removed. The structure and outcomes are close to the original.

## Client and context

A national water technology company has approached the Faculty of Business and Law about a professional development program for its finance and operations leaders.

The request came out of a consultation between the client's finance lead and the university's Executive Education team. The client wants a program that helps operational managers read the numbers they already receive, and make better decisions with them.

## Stated outcomes

The client has listed these outcomes for the program:

- Understand a balance sheet: what story does it tell us?
- Understand asset value: how are assets recognised, and how do they affect the overall financial position?
- Understand inventory: what is the problem with large inventory at hand, and what are the principles and benefits of just-in-time control?
- Understand aging assets: how are they managed or written down over time, and what are the implications?
- Understand a balanced scorecard and how it is built and used.
- Develop a plan on a page for the next financial year: what needs to be considered from a financial and strategic perspective?
- Operationalise strategy from a financial implication perspective.

## Proposed shape

A two-day program, configured to suit the availability of the client's leadership team.

### Day 1: understanding the financial story

| Module | Focus |
|---|---|
| Program outline | Structure, topics, participant introductions |
| 1. The balance sheet story | The balance sheet as a leadership tool. Assets, inventory, receivables, liabilities and working capital. How operational decisions show up in the financial position. |
| 2. The profit and loss story | Revenue, costs, margins and earnings. How pricing, productivity, service delivery, maintenance, procurement and resource use affect performance. |
| 3. The cash flow story | Liquidity, financial discipline and sustainability. The link between profit, cash flow and working capital. |

### Day 2: creating financial value

| Module | Focus |
|---|---|
| 4. Creating financial value through operational decisions | How the three statements work together. Facilitated scenarios and case discussion. Evaluating alternative decisions and their financial implications. |
| 5. Executive business challenge | Team-based business case. Analyse the issue, evaluate options, recommend a course of action, present with evidence, reflect. |

## What the client has not told us

- Which parts of the business the participants come from, and their current financial literacy.
- Whether the client wants its own data used in the exercises, and whether that data can be shared.
- Whether the program is assessed or certificate-bearing.
- Preferred dates and location.
