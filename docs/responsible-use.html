Session 2 · The rules in plain language

Responsible use

This is not legal advice. It is the team’s operating habit, aligned to Curtin policy. If a decision matters, check the current Curtin policy or ask the faculty’s data governance contact.

The short version

Only put into Copilot what you would put into a Curtin system. Treat every output as a draft by an unknown author. A person signs off, and the person who sends it owns it.

What enterprise Copilot protects

Curtin’s Copilot runs inside the Curtin tenant. Prompts, responses and the work data it can reach stay within the organisation’s Microsoft 365 environment, under the same contractual terms as mail and files. That is a real protection, and it is why the tool is approved.

It does not make every document appropriate to paste. Confidentiality, privacy and client obligations still apply.

What not to paste

  • Client or participant personal information, unless there is a clear reason and it is within policy.
  • Pricing, margins, contract terms and anything covered by a confidentiality clause.
  • Third-party material the client has not cleared for this use.
  • Anything you would not put in a Curtin system.
  • Anything you could not defend if the client saw the prompt.

When in doubt, use a sanitised brief, or ask before you paste.

What Copilot gets wrong

  • Fluency is not evidence. It writes confident sentences about things it cannot see.
  • It agrees with you. Push back and it folds. Ask for the strongest case against its own answer.
  • It invents specifics. Names, dates, figures, citations, policy clauses. Check every one.
  • It uses stale context. A document from last year, an old brief, an out-of-date fee schedule.
  • It loses the thread between apps. The outline in Word does not know what you agreed in Outlook.

Human checkpoints

These do not move, whatever the tool does:

  • A person reads anything that goes to a client, end to end.
  • A person checks every figure against a source.
  • A person owns every commitment, date and price.
  • A person decides what is filed as a record.

Records and audit

If an output becomes a Curtin record, it follows the same record-keeping rules as any other document. Copilot activity is logged in the tenant. Do not treat a chat as private note-taking for anything that matters.

Memory and personalisation

Copilot remembers context to be useful. Review it. Delete anything wrong, stale or about a client. If you share a machine or a demo account, check what is remembered before you present.

A five-line check before you send

  1. Does it name the right client, program and people?
  2. Is every number one I can trace?
  3. Did it invent a commitment, a date or a claim?
  4. Is the tone right for this reader?
  5. Would I be comfortable if the client saw how this was produced?
