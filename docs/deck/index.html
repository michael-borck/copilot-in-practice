The problem

Ask Copilot about your email.

Same account. Same day. Two different answers.
Copilot Chat · browser or Edge
"I don't have access to your inbox. You can paste the email here."
Web context plus whatever you give it.
Copilot in Outlook · pane open
"Here is what I can see in this thread: three decisions the client is asking for..."
The email you selected is the context.
That gap is the session. Not a limitation to wait out. A place where you decide what Copilot sees.
The one idea

You are the orchestrator.

OUTLOOK

Gather

The email, the thread, the calendar. This is where the context lives.

CHAT

Extract

Ask for the decisions, dates and asks. Keep it short.

WORD

Refine

Paste it, say where it came from, ask for the shape you need.

EXCEL

Quantify

Turn scope into a table, rates to fill, a formula to check.

YOU

Carry and check

Every arrow is a copy and paste by a person. That is where judgement enters.

Copilot does not pass work between apps for you. You do. That is the skill.

Risk

Where it trips people up.

  • It agrees with you. Push back and it folds. That is sycophancy, not agreement.
  • It invents specifics. Names, dates, figures, citations, policy clauses. Fluency is not evidence.
  • It uses stale context. Last year's brief, an old fee schedule, a superseded program.
  • It loses the thread between apps. The outline in Word does not know what you agreed in Outlook.
  • It writes confidently about what it cannot see. A blank space becomes a plausible guess.
The trade-offs

It moves the friction. It does not remove it.

TensionIf you lean too farWhat to remember
Thinking vs replacingYou stop forming your own viewUse it to support reasoning, not bypass it
Speed vs safetyData leaks, thin work goes outStart with safe material and the approved tool
One-shot vs conversationSuperficial, brittle outputMost good work needs a second and third turn
Tool vs workflowShiny object, no lasting gainThe workflow is the win, not the tool

The rules, in plain language

What may go in.

Usually fine
Work you would put in a Curtin system
Sanitised briefs, public program descriptions, your own notes with names removed, generic drafts.
Needs a reason
Client and participant detail
Personal information, pricing, margins, contract terms. Use the minimum, know why, stay within policy.
Keep out
Cleared material and confidentiality
Third-party material the client has not cleared, and anything you could not defend if the client saw the prompt.
Copilot is the official tool. Claude is by application. Public tools are for mock data only.
The line that does not move
Copilot can draft. It cannot be accountable. That stays with the person whose name is on the email.

The habit

Carry the work on purpose.

01 GATHER

Open the source

Select the email. Open the document. Open the page.

02 EXTRACT

Ask for the bones

Decisions, dates, asks, numbers. Short.

03 CARRY

Paste it across

Say where it came from. Do not assume the next app knows.

04 REFINE

Shape it

Ask for the format the destination needs.

05 CHECK

Verify

Names, figures, commitments. Before it moves on.

Every arrow is a copy and paste by a person. That is not a bug. That is control.
The browser

Edge is a context too.

  • Sign in with the Curtin account. A personal account gives you the consumer experience.
  • Work mode and web mode are different. Check which one you are in before you ask.
  • Uploading a file is a deliberate act. Confirm the file, the permissions and the audience.
  • The open page is context. Close anything you would not want summarised if you share your screen.
The brief

RTCF.

Role, task, context, format. Context does most of the work.
R
Role
Who should write this. "An experienced proposal writer who writes plainly."
T
Task
The verb and the scope. "Draft a one-page proposal outline."
C
Context
The reader, the situation, the standard, and the source pasted in.
F
Format
Length, structure, tone.
Paste the source. Do not describe it.
When it misses

Four upgrades.

01 EXAMPLE

Match one you liked

Paste it. Match the shape, not the content.

02 DONE

Define success

Length, structure, facts only. Mark guesses with [CHECK].

03 QUESTIONS

Let it ask first

Up to five questions, one at a time, before it starts.

04 STEPS

Split the job

Outline first, then one section at a time.

Steps is also how you batch a workflow into one prompt. It still does not join the apps.

Exercise 18 minutes

Read the brief like a proposal writer.

  • The client's stated outcomes, in their words.
  • What they need but did not say.
  • The three decisions the proposal must help them make.
  • The questions we should ask before writing.
Prompt
Read this brief. Give me: 1. stated outcomes, in their words 2. what they need but did not say 3. the three decisions 4. questions to ask Mark anything you inferred. BRIEF: [paste]
OutputA short brief analysis you can carry into Word.
Exercise 215 minutes

Draft the outline in Word.

  • Paste the brief and your analysis into Word.
  • Ask for the house structure: context, outcomes, modules, delivery, fees, why Curtin.
  • Second pass: cut anything generic. Keep only what is specific.
  • Mark what it inferred rather than read.
Second prompt
Cut anything that would be true of any provider. Keep only what is specific to this brief. Mark inferences with [inferred].
OutputA draft outline, clearly marked where it guessed.
Exercise 314 minutes

Get an honest critique.

  • Ask Copilot to be a sceptical client.
  • Find the unsupported claims and the unanswered questions.
  • Ask for a revision plan, not a rewrite.
  • Revise one section by hand.
Prompt
Act as a sceptical client. What makes you doubt this provider? Which claims are unsupported? Where does it answer a question I did not ask? What is missing against the criteria? Give a revision plan, not a rewrite. OUTLINE: [paste]
OutputA revision plan, and one section improved by you.
Exercise 45 minutes

Turn scope into a table.

  • Paste the scope list into Excel or Chat.
  • Ask for activity, assumption, quantity, rate, total.
  • Leave rates blank. Do not let it invent a price.
  • Ask it to explain the total formula in plain English.
Prompt
Propose an Excel table: activity, assumption, quantity, rate, total. Leave rate blank. Give the total formula in plain English, then the formula. Do not invent rates. SCOPE: [paste]
OutputA table you can fill, and a formula you understand.

Save it

Build a prompt you can reuse.

ROLE: [who should write this] TASK: [verb and scope] CONTEXT: [reader, situation, standard, source] FORMAT: [length, structure, tone] CHECK: [what a person must verify] NEVER: [what it must not invent or include]
Save it where you already work: a note, a Word file, a saved prompt if your licence has them.
Keep the judgement

Name the checkpoints.

  • Before anything goes to a client.
  • Before any figure is quoted.
  • Before any commitment or date is made.
  • Before anything is filed as a record.
It works every week when the checkpoints are named and the handoffs are deliberate.
