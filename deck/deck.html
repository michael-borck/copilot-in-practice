Welcome. Set the frame in one line: this morning is about getting real work done with the tools you already have, not about becoming technical.

Ask for a show of hands: who has opened Copilot Chat and asked about an email? Keep the answers for the next slide. The room's own experience is the curriculum.

The problem

Ask Copilot about your email.

Same account. Same day. Two different answers.
Copilot Chat · browser or Edge
"I don't have access to your inbox. You can paste the email here."
Web context plus whatever you give it.
Copilot in Outlook · pane open
"Here is what I can see in this thread: three decisions the client is asking for..."
The email you selected is the context.
That gap is the session. Not a limitation to wait out. A place where you decide what Copilot sees.

Run this live if you can. Open Copilot Chat, ask about the inbox, then open an email in Outlook and ask the same question in the pane. The room will see the difference for themselves.

If someone says "mine can see my inbox", ask which licence they have. That is the bridge to Session 3.

This slide is the spine of the morning. Say it plainly: the tools are not joined up, and that is fine. The person is the integration layer.

Keep this tied to their week. Ask which of these six they did last week. The answer shapes the exercises.

The pattern: Copilot is strong at first drafts, restructuring, summarising and critique. It is weak at knowing your client, your numbers and your commitments.

Risk

Where it trips people up.

  • It agrees with you. Push back and it folds. That is sycophancy, not agreement.
  • It invents specifics. Names, dates, figures, citations, policy clauses. Fluency is not evidence.
  • It uses stale context. Last year's brief, an old fee schedule, a superseded program.
  • It loses the thread between apps. The outline in Word does not know what you agreed in Outlook.
  • It writes confidently about what it cannot see. A blank space becomes a plausible guess.

Do not read these out as a list of warnings. Ask the room which one they have already seen. Most will have a story about a confident wrong answer.

Hold the sycophancy point for the live demo on the next slide.

The trade-offs

It moves the friction. It does not remove it.

TensionIf you lean too farWhat to remember
Thinking vs replacingYou stop forming your own viewUse it to support reasoning, not bypass it
Speed vs safetyData leaks, thin work goes outStart with safe material and the approved tool
One-shot vs conversationSuperficial, brittle outputMost good work needs a second and third turn
Tool vs workflowShiny object, no lasting gainThe workflow is the win, not the tool

Do not spend long here. Name the four, ask which one they have felt, and move on. The point is to make the trade-off visible, not to resolve it.

The last row is the one that matters for this team: the workflow is the win.

Live demo

Watch it switch sides.

You
Here is our plan for the new program. Justify it to the client.
"This is a strong proposal. The structure clearly addresses the client's priorities..."
You, pushing back
I think this plan is weak and the client will hate it.
"You are right. This plan has significant gaps. The client may see..."
Same plan. Opposite answer. The prompt did not change the facts. It changed the audience.

Run it live with a real (sanitised) outline. Ask for a justification, then say "actually I think this is weak". Watch the reversal.

Then teach the counter-prompt: "Before you answer, list the two strongest reasons this might be wrong. Do not agree with me by default."

If it does not flip, even better. Ask why, and use that to discuss how reliably you can trust the tone.

The rules, in plain language

What may go in.

Usually fine
Work you would put in a Curtin system
Sanitised briefs, public program descriptions, your own notes with names removed, generic drafts.
Needs a reason
Client and participant detail
Personal information, pricing, margins, contract terms. Use the minimum, know why, stay within policy.
Keep out
Cleared material and confidentiality
Third-party material the client has not cleared, and anything you could not defend if the client saw the prompt.
Copilot is the official tool. Claude is by application. Public tools are for mock data only.

Mention that enterprise Copilot keeps data in the tenant. That is a real protection. It does not make every document appropriate to paste.

The line that does not move
Copilot can draft. It cannot be accountable. That stays with the person whose name is on the email.

Pause here. Let it land. Then ask: what would you never let it send without reading? Their answers are the checkpoints for Session 5.

The browser

Edge is a context too.

  • Sign in with the Curtin account. A personal account gives you the consumer experience.
  • Work mode and web mode are different. Check which one you are in before you ask.
  • Uploading a file is a deliberate act. Confirm the file, the permissions and the audience.
  • The open page is context. Close anything you would not want summarised if you share your screen.

Keep this short. Most of the room lives in Edge already. The teaching point is that the browser is not neutral: what is open becomes context.

The brief

RTCF.

Role, task, context, format. Microsoft's four elements, plus a role.
R
Role
Who should write this. "An experienced proposal writer who writes plainly."
T
Task
The verb and the scope. "Draft a one-page proposal outline."
C
Context
The reader, the situation, the standard, and the source pasted in.
F
Format
Length, structure, tone.
Paste the source. Do not describe it.

RTCF is the whole of prompt craft for this audience. Context is the corner that does the work, so make it the one they never skip.

Point them to the prompt library on the site. They will use it more than they will remember this slide.

When it misses

Four upgrades.

01 EXAMPLE

Match one you liked

Paste it. Match the shape, not the content.

02 DONE

Define success

Length, structure, facts only. Mark guesses with [CHECK].

03 QUESTIONS

Let it ask first

Up to five questions, one at a time, before it starts.

04 STEPS

Split the job

Outline first, then one section at a time.

Steps is also how you batch a workflow into one prompt. It still does not join the apps.

Ask which upgrade they would add to their own task. Most will pick Example or Done.

Be honest that Steps runs in one context. If the work needs the email, put the email in. Do not assume the next app knows.

Demo3 minutes

Fix a broken formula.

  • Open a spreadsheet with a formula that will not work.
  • Ask Copilot what is wrong and how to fix it.
  • Ask it to explain the fix in plain English.
  • Ask who has a formula that has cost them an afternoon.
Prompt
What is wrong with this formula, and how do I fix it? Explain the fix in plain English, as if I have to maintain it next year. FORMULA: [paste]
OutputThe fastest win in the room. Carry it into Exercise 4.

This is the demo that lands hardest with non-technical teams. Use a real broken formula if you have one.

Do not fix it silently. Narrate the prompt and the explanation, then ask how they would check it.

Exercise 1 · Find8 minutes

Read the brief like a proposal writer.

  • The client's stated outcomes, in their words.
  • What they need but did not say.
  • The three decisions the proposal must help them make.
  • The questions we should ask before writing.
Prompt
Read this brief. Give me: 1. stated outcomes, in their words 2. what they need but did not say 3. the three decisions 4. questions to ask Mark anything you inferred. BRIEF: [paste]
OutputA short brief analysis you can carry into Word.

Pairs or threes. Use the proposal brief for a gentler start, the tender brief for a stretch.

Compare answers across pairs after seven minutes. The differences show how much the pasted context and the prompt wording matter.

Exercise 2 · Draft15 minutes

Draft the outline in Word.

  • Paste the brief and your analysis into Word.
  • Ask for the house structure: context, outcomes, modules, delivery, fees, why Curtin.
  • Second pass: cut anything generic. Keep only what is specific.
  • Mark what it inferred rather than read.
Second prompt
Cut anything that would be true of any provider. Keep only what is specific to this brief. Mark inferences with [inferred].
OutputA draft outline, clearly marked where it guessed.

The first output will be generic. That is useful. The second prompt is the lesson: specificity comes from the source, not from asking for "more detail".

Watch for invented credentials, invented modules and invented fees. Point them out in the debrief.

Exercise 3 · Challenge14 minutes

Get an honest critique.

  • Ask Copilot to be a sceptical client.
  • Find the unsupported claims and the unanswered questions.
  • Ask for a revision plan, not a rewrite.
  • Revise one section by hand.
Prompt
Act as a sceptical client. What makes you doubt this provider? Which claims are unsupported? Where does it answer a question I did not ask? What is missing against the criteria? Give a revision plan, not a rewrite. OUTLINE: [paste]
OutputA revision plan, and one section improved by you.

The critique is the valuable output, not the rewrite. If they let Copilot rewrite, the document drifts toward generic.

Ask one pair to read their critique aloud. Usually someone finds a claim that would not survive a client reading it.

Exercise 4 · Quantify5 minutes

Turn scope into a table.

  • Paste the scope list into Excel or Chat.
  • Ask for activity, assumption, quantity, rate, total.
  • Leave rates blank. Do not let it invent a price.
  • Ask it to explain the total formula in plain English.
Prompt
Propose an Excel table: activity, assumption, quantity, rate, total. Leave rate blank. Give the total formula in plain English, then the formula. Do not invent rates. SCOPE: [paste]
OutputA table you can fill, and a formula you understand.

This is the one place where invented numbers are most dangerous. Make the point: a plausible rate is worse than a blank cell.

If time is short, demo this rather than have everyone do it.

Make it yours

Map one task you do every week.

Input
What starts it. An enquiry, a brief, a meeting, a date.
Now
What you do today, in order.
Pain
Where it slows down or goes wrong.
Draft + check
Where Copilot drafts, and where a person checks.
Candidates: enquiry to proposal, post-program follow-up, marketing calendar, BD pipeline, tender response.

Give them two minutes of silent thinking, then pair share. Most people pick the same two or three tasks. That is fine, and it makes the follow-up easier.

Save it

Build a prompt you can reuse.

ROLE: [who should write this] TASK: [verb and scope] CONTEXT: [reader, situation, standard, source] FORMAT: [length, structure, tone] CHECK: [what a person must verify] NEVER: [what it must not invent or include]
Save it where you already work: a note, a Word file, a saved prompt if your licence has them.

The "Never" line does a lot of work. It is where they encode their professional judgement: no invented pricing, no client names, no commitments without approval.

Keep the judgement

Name the checkpoints.

  • Before anything goes to a client.
  • Before any figure is quoted.
  • Before any commitment or date is made.
  • Before anything is filed as a record.
It works every week when the checkpoints are named and the handoffs are deliberate.

Ask each person to say their checkpoint out loud. Public commitment beats a worksheet.

Optional · 5 minutes

Agents, briefly.

An agent is a brief that remembers.
01 · Already there
Built-in agents
Analyst, Researcher, Facilitator, Writing Coach, Idea Coach. You use them, you do not build them.
02 · You build it
A custom agent
Instructions plus files. The Custom GPT equivalent. Same RTCF brief, saved with a name.
03 · A project
Copilot Studio
Actions and systems. Needs an owner, a knowledge source and governance. Out of scope today.
Do not automate a step you have not done manually twice. The agent saves clicks, not judgement.

Optional. If time is short, skip the slide and say the line: an agent is a brief that remembers.

If someone asks about building one, map it: the instructions are the RTCF prompt, the knowledge is the files, the conversation is the iteration. Sharing is blocked on the tenant, so it stays personal.
